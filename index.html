        Notes
        1. The turbulence box is an estimate from a weather model and the plane's own data, not pilot reports. Turbulence is uncomfortable, but airliners are built to handle far more than passengers feel.
        2. Map: pinch or double-tap to zoom in, tap with two fingers to zoom out, or double-tap and hold then drag up to zoom in and down to zoom out; on a computer, Ctrl + scroll or a trackpad pinch zooms the map; drag to move once zoomed. Wind streaks show the air's direction at your altitude; amber marks stronger shear. Traffic shows up to 40 aircraft within 60 miles and 8,000 ft of you in flight; below 10,000 ft it shows up to 80, including aircraft on the ground. Tap one for details. Zoom in on an airport to see its runways, taxiways and gates (from OpenStreetMap) and which one you're on.
        3. The heading trace spirals outward over time: the center is when tracking began and the edge is now.
        4. Time to go assumes your current speed on a direct path. Clock times use your phone's time zone.
        5. Aircraft details and photos come from volunteer databases (adsbdb, planespotters.net, OpenSky) and can be missing or out of date. Past flights come from OpenSky and can lag by a day. No free source has an aircraft's lifetime flight count or the factory that built it.
        6. Positions come from community ADS-B receiver networks (adsb.lol, airplanes.live, adsb.fi, OpenSky), through a public relay if your browser blocks the direct request. Winds are from Open-Meteo. State outlines are US Census data. For curiosity only, not for navigation.
        7. Data sources: the top four supply your plane's live position; the page asks all four at once and uses the best one that has your flight. Winds is the weather model behind the wind streaks and turbulence estimate. Traffic is the nearby-aircraft lookup. History fetches the part of this flight flown before the page was opened or while it was offline, to fill in the charts and the flown path. Your relay is your own Cloudflare Worker, kept as a backup: it is only used when the other routes have failed for 15 seconds, and the page goes back to the free routes when they work again. Airport map loads taxiways, aprons and gates from OpenStreetMap when you zoom in on an airport; if it can't, the map still shows the runways from built-in data. Green = working, red square = every route failed, hollow ring = reachable but your flight isn't there, amber = trying.
